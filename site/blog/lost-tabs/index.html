Are you losing browser tabs in your tab manager?

You had sixty tabs open, you closed Chrome or it crashed, and when you came back half of them were gone. If you use a tab manager and tabs still go missing, you are not unlucky. Losing tabs was the most common complaint in our research of what people say about tab managers: it came up in 22 of the 64 Reddit threads we looked at. (Those threads were summarised by an AI from search results, so read the number as a rough guide, not a count of people.)

Why tabs go missing

  • A crash or a forced restart. Chrome can only bring tabs back if it saved them first. After a hard crash or a power cut, that last save may be old or missing.
  • Chrome is set to open a new tab page. Unless it is set to continue where you left off, a normal quit leaves nothing to restore.
  • A single saved list. Some tab managers collapse everything into one list. If that list is the only copy, a bug, a reinstall or removing the extension can take it with it.
  • An extension that changes hands. Tab extensions have been sold and then changed or abused. The data you trusted to one is only as safe as its owner.
  • Closing a window by accident. Closing is instant, and it is easy to do it to the wrong window.

What to do today, with or without BetterTabs

  1. In Chrome, open Settings, then On startup, and choose Continue where you left off.
  2. Learn the undo: Ctrl/Cmd + Shift + T reopens the last closed tab, and keeps going back, one tab or window at a time.
  3. Check Chrome's History for Recently closed if a whole window disappeared.
  4. Before anything risky (an update, a reinstall, a big cleanup), save a copy of what is open as a file you keep yourself.
  5. Do not let one extension be your only copy of anything you care about.

What BetterTabs does about it

BetterTabs is a list of your open tabs, not a place where tabs are stored, so closing it or removing it never deletes a tab. It adds some protection:

  • Save backup to a file. In the settings, one click saves your favourites, saved lists, tab names, settings and recent tabs to a file you keep, and Restore from a file brings them back, even after removing and reinstalling the extension.
  • Recent tabs. Kept automatically, with nothing to turn on. A copy of your open tabs stays on your computer, a new one about every five minutes while they change, and Restore from a file in the settings opens a copy as new windows. It skips pages you already have open and loads only the first tab of each window, so hundreds of tabs don't load at once. You can delete the copies; nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Names survive a restart, and favourites survive closing the tab. The names you gave tabs come back when the same page is open again, saved under a short hash of the page address, never the address itself. A favourite stays in the row above the search box after you close its tab, and one click opens the page again.
  • Reopen closed tab still works. Closing in BetterTabs is Chrome's own close, so Chrome's undo brings the tab back with its name.
  • Nothing leaves your browser. There is no account and no server, so there is no company between you and your tabs.

What it does not do yet

BetterTabs now has saved lists too: tick tabs, choose Save list, and open the list later from the settings. A list is only as safe as the computer it is on, so keep a backup file as well. A list holds only what you chose to save. The one thing kept without you choosing is your recent tabs, which stay on your computer for about half a day and which you can delete at any time.

The short version

Turn on Continue where you left off, remember the reopen shortcut, and export a copy before anything risky. Keep your tabs listed by something that cannot lose them, and your backup in a file that is yours.
